Why KaziLink

Bridging the gap between skills and opportunity

Young people may have valuable skills, ideas, training, and motivation, yet struggle to access professional networks, practical experience, references, or information about work opportunities.

These barriers can affect both refugees and members of the host community. KaziLink proposes a practical way to help participants demonstrate what they can do and connect with people and organizations that can support their next steps.

Our goal

Improve access to professional networks, practical experience, and employment opportunities for refugee and host-community youth in Kakuma–Kalobeyei.

Who the pilot is for

The proposed pilot will focus on young people aged 18–30, including first-time job seekers, people with skills but limited work experience, graduates or trainees, and people returning to work.

How it works

Five connected parts

KaziLink combines human support with a simple, accessible way to document skills and connect people to opportunities.

1

Skills assessment

Understand each participant’s strengths, interests, experience, and goals.

2

Mentorship

Connect participants with mentors for guidance, feedback, and career planning.

3

Practical experience

Develop supervised projects, assignments, fellowships, or other learning opportunities.

4

Skills-based profiles

Help participants present skills, training, projects, and experience in a clear profile.

5

Opportunity matching

Connect participants with relevant employers, organizations, interviews, and projects.

+

Local partnerships

Work with community organizations, training providers, businesses, and potential hiring partners.

Proposed pilot

A six-month path from preparation to learning

The pilot is designed to start small, consult locally, test the model, and use participant and partner feedback to guide future development.

The numbers and activities below are planning proposals. They are not confirmed commitments, partnerships, or achieved results.
Month 1

Listen and prepare

Consult the community, contact potential partners, set up the project, and recruit participants.

Month 2

Assess and orient

Assess skills, recruit and orient mentors, and introduce participants to the project.

Month 3

Begin mentorship

Match mentors and participants and develop skills-based profiles and career plans.

Month 4

Build experience

Begin practical assignments and match participants to relevant opportunities.

Month 5

Connect to employers

Continue mentorship and support referrals, interviews, and partner engagement.

Month 6

Review and improve

Collect feedback, assess outcomes, and plan whether and how to continue.

What success could look like

Proposed pilot targets

KaziLink will track progress and learn from results. Targets will be reviewed after local consultation and confirmation of resources.

  • 50 participants enrolled
  • 15 mentors recruited
  • 5 employer or organizational partners engaged
  • 40 participants complete mentorship
  • 30 participants receive an interview or opportunity referral
  • 15 participants access employment, paid assignments, or another verified work-related outcome
Responsible implementation

Safety, fairness, and respect matter

The project will aim to protect participants’ dignity, privacy, and choice throughout recruitment, mentorship, and opportunity matching.

Key safeguards

  • Collect only necessary participant information and obtain consent before sharing profiles.
  • Offer offline or assisted access for people with limited devices or connectivity.
  • Use clear expectations, supervision, and safeguarding procedures for practical placements.
  • Avoid using unpaid placements to replace paid staff; clarify compensation and conditions in advance.
  • Use transparent, inclusive participant selection criteria.
Long-term vision

Start locally. Learn. Build responsibly.

KaziLink will begin as a small pilot. Its future may be supported by local partnerships, mentor networks, employer participation, and funding from grants or social-impact programs.

What the project needs to move forward

  • Community consultation and a local implementation partner.
  • Mentors and organizations willing to explore collaboration.
  • A realistic budget and confirmed resources.
  • Clear participant eligibility, privacy, and safeguarding arrangements.
  • A simple process for monitoring progress and learning from feedback.
